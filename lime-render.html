What it is

A render that lets old walls breathe

Lime render is made from lime, sand and water. It sets slowly and stays slightly flexible, so it moves with the building instead of cracking away from it.

It is the right choice for most period properties, cottages and listed buildings with solid stone or brick walls. Unlike cement render, it lets moisture in the wall dry out, which helps prevent damp and decay.

Why choose it

Why clients choose Lime render

01

Breathable

Moisture in solid walls can escape through the render rather than being trapped behind it.

02

Flexible

Lime moves with older buildings, so it is less prone to cracking than hard cement render.

03

04

A gentle texture and colour that suits older properties, and can be finished with limewash.

FAQs

Lime render questions, answered

Is lime render right for my house?

It usually is if the house has solid stone or brick walls, which most homes built before the 1920s do. Modern cavity walls built from blockwork often suit other renders. We check the walls at survey and recommend the right system.

Can lime render be painted?

Yes, but it needs a breathable finish such as limewash or a mineral paint. Standard masonry paints can seal the surface and trap moisture.

Does lime render need special weather?

Lime sets slowly, so it needs protecting from frost, heavy rain and strong sun while it cures. That makes spring to early autumn the best time for lime work.
